Understanding equity release

What is equity release?

Equity release allows homeowners aged 55+ to access money tied up in their property without having to move. The most common option is a lifetime mortgage, which lets you release a tax-free lump sum or take smaller amounts over time.

You remain the legal owner of your home and the loan, plus any interest, is usually repaid when your property is sold — normally when you pass away or move into long-term care. At Bay Mortgages, we explain the benefits, risks and alternatives clearly, so you can decide whether equity release is genuinely right for your situation.

Stay in your home

You keep legal ownership and can usually remain in your property for life.

Flexible options

You may be able to choose a lump sum, drawdown facility or other later-life borrowing route.

Built-in protections

We only recommend plans that meet the right standards and include important safeguards such as no negative equity protection.

Why people consider equity release

Common uses of equity release

Equity release is designed to be flexible. People use it for very different reasons depending on their stage of life, family circumstances and financial priorities.

Home improvements

Renovate, adapt or improve your home, whether that means a new kitchen, essential repairs or making the property more suitable for later life.

Clear mortgage or debts

Repay an interest-only mortgage or reduce unsecured debts, helping to ease financial pressure and improve monthly affordability.

Support family

Help children or grandchildren with a house deposit, education costs, weddings or gifting part of an inheritance earlier in life.

Boost retirement income

Create extra financial flexibility in retirement, whether that is for day-to-day comfort, travel, one-off costs or added peace of mind.

Questions people often ask

Frequently asked questions

We've answered some of the most common questions about equity release below. If you want tailored advice, we're always happy to talk things through with you.

Will I still own my home?

Yes. With a lifetime mortgage, which is the most common form of equity release, you remain the legal owner of your property. The lender simply places a charge against it, which is usually repaid when you pass away or move into long-term care.

What if I want to move house later?

Many plans allow you to move to another suitable property and transfer the mortgage with you. If the new property does not meet the lender's criteria, the loan may need to be repaid, although some plans include downsizing protection.

Can I pay off equity release early?

Some plans allow voluntary repayments without penalty, while others may charge an early repayment fee. If flexibility matters to you, we can help you choose a product that reflects that from the start.

How will equity release affect my family?

Releasing equity reduces the value of your estate, which may affect inheritance. Some plans allow part of the property value to be ring-fenced, and many clients choose to involve family members in the conversation so everyone understands the decision.

Is equity release safe?

Yes, provided you use a regulated adviser and a lender that follows the right standards. Important safeguards can include the no negative equity guarantee and the right to remain in your home for life.
